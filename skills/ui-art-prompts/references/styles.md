## 1. Luminist landscape
**Mood:** calm, trustworthy, timeless, quietly premium. The "serious tool with a soul" look (the Untitled UI hero aesthetic).
**Vocabulary:** "19th-century American luminist landscape oil painting", "Hudson River School", "golden hour", "glassy still water", "atmospheric haze", "distant mountains dissolving into mist", "aged varnish patina".
**Palette words:** cream, warm ochre, soft amber, sage green, faded terracotta, hazy blue-grey.
**Quiet zones that come naturally:** sky (upper half), still water — both render smooth and low-contrast.
**Seats:** auth backdrops, landing heroes with dark UI panels on top.

## 2. Impressionist
**Mood:** lively, human, optimistic. Warmer and busier than luminism.
**Vocabulary:** "French impressionist oil painting", "loose broken brushstrokes", "dappled light", "plein air", "Monet-era garden scene".
**Palette words:** wisteria lavender, chalky pink, spring green, pale gold.
**Caution:** high stroke energy everywhere — carve the quiet zone explicitly ("soft out-of-focus expanse of pale sky") or UI will sit on visual noise.
**Seats:** marketing pages, lifestyle/consumer products, empty states.

## 3. Japanese woodblock / ukiyo-e
**Mood:** serene, disciplined, crafted. Flat color reads beautifully behind UI.
**Vocabulary:** "Japanese ukiyo-e woodblock print", "Hokusai and Hiroshige style", "flat planes of color", "visible woodgrain texture", "bokashi gradient sky".
**Palette words:** indigo, prussian blue, persimmon, sea-foam, warm paper tone.
**Quiet zone trick:** "large area of unprinted warm paper tone" — genuinely flat, perfect under buttons or forms.
**Seats:** onboarding, meditation/wellness, anything wanting calm order.

## 4. Ink wash
**Mood:** contemplative, minimal, scholarly.
**Vocabulary:** "traditional Chinese shan shui ink wash painting" or "Japanese sumi-e", "diluted black ink on rice paper", "vast negative space", "mist between mountain layers", "a few confident brushstrokes".
**Palette words:** ink black through pale grey washes, warm rice-paper white; optionally one accent ("a single seal-red accent").
**Strength:** negative space is the *subject* — the easiest style to get a clean quiet zone from.
**Seats:** empty states, focus/writing tools, dark-mode-adjacent designs (invert: "white ink on deep charcoal paper").

## 5. Watercolor
**Mood:** gentle, personal, hand-made.
**Vocabulary:** "loose watercolor painting", "wet-on-wet blooms", "soft bleeding edges", "white of the paper showing through", "granulating pigment texture".
**Palette words:** name 3–4 max; watercolor muddies fast. E.g. "sage, dusty rose, warm grey".
**Caution:** edges dissolve — great for vignetted spot illustrations on white/cream, poor for full-bleed edge-to-edge cover.
**Seats:** empty states, onboarding spot art, food/recipe/journal apps.

## 6. Gouache / mid-century travel poster
**Mood:** confident, friendly, a little nostalgic-modern.
**Vocabulary:** "mid-century travel poster in gouache", "flat matte shapes", "simplified stylized landscape", "screen-print texture", "1950s national park poster style".
**Palette words:** mustard, teal, cream, burnt orange, olive.
**Strength:** flat shapes = predictable contrast; easy to match brand hexes almost literally.
**Seats:** landing heroes, feature sections, product with personality (dev tools included).

## 7. Art deco
**Mood:** luxurious, geometric, theatrical.
**Vocabulary:** "art deco poster illustration", "bold geometric symmetry", "sunburst motifs", "stepped forms", "gold leaf accents", "1920s luxury".
**Palette words:** deep emerald or midnight navy ground, champagne gold, ivory.
**Caution:** strong symmetry fights off-center UI — either center the UI or ask for "ornamental border framing a calm dark central field".
**Seats:** premium/fintech landing pages, invitations, dark heroes.

## 8. Dutch Golden Age still life
**Mood:** rich, serious, editorial. Dramatic dark ground is a ready-made seat for light text.
**Vocabulary:** "Dutch Golden Age still life oil painting", "chiaroscuro", "single window light from the left", "deep near-black background", "vanitas arrangement", "glazed oil depth".
**Palette words:** near-black umber ground, deep crimson, brass, honey, ivory highlights.
**Quiet zone:** the dark ground itself — ask for the arrangement "occupying the lower right third" and the rest stays near-black.
**Seats:** editorial heroes, premium food/wine, dark-theme products.

## 9. Botanical / naturalist illustration
**Mood:** precise, calm, trustworthy-scientific.
**Vocabulary:** "vintage botanical illustration", "hand-colored engraving", "natural history plate", "fine linework", "aged cream paper".
**Palette words:** cream paper ground, moss green, madder red, sepia ink.
**Strength:** subjects float on flat paper ground — trivially clean quiet zones; tiles well as a pattern.
**Seats:** empty states, health/wellness, docs/knowledge products, decorative corners.

## 10. Color field / abstract
**Mood:** modern, emotional, gallery-quiet. When you want painterly *feeling* with zero subject matter.
**Vocabulary:** "abstract color field oil painting", "Rothko-inspired soft-edged rectangles of color", "subtle tonal transitions", "matte canvas texture", or "gestural minimal abstract, two calm brushstrokes on a warm ground".
**Palette words:** exactly the brand palette, plus a ground ("warm off-white ground").
**Strength:** the entire image is quiet zone. Safest choice when UI covers most of the image.
**Seats:** full-bleed backgrounds, section dividers, brand moments.

## 11. Fresco / classical mural
**Mood:** grand, humanist, aged. Renaissance-institution energy.
**Vocabulary:** "Renaissance fresco painting", "faded plaster texture", "cracked patina", "muted earth pigments", "classical drapery and architecture softened by age".
**Palette words:** plaster white, terracotta, faded cerulean, ochre.
**Note:** the "worn away" quality is the feature — ask for "large weathered areas where the plaster shows through" as quiet zones.
**Seats:** education, humanities, editorial brands, hero backgrounds.

## 12. Nocturne / tonalism
**Mood:** hushed, intimate, premium-dark. The dark-mode of paintings.
**Vocabulary:** "tonalist nocturne oil painting", "Whistler-style nocturne", "moonlit harbor", "deep blue-grey atmosphere", "a few distant warm lights", "forms dissolving into darkness".
**Palette words:** deep slate blue, charcoal, silver moonlight, one warm amber light source.
**Strength:** naturally low contrast and dark — light text and glowing UI panels sit beautifully on it.
**Seats:** dark-theme auth screens, night/sleep/focus products, dramatic heroes.

## Matching brand hexes to pigment language

Generators don't reliably honor hex codes; translate them:

| Hex family | Say instead |
|---|---|
| Warm yellows/oranges (#E9A94A) | golden ochre, warm amber, honey |
| Muted greens (#448361) | sage, viridian, moss |
| Desaturated blues (#337EA9) | slate blue, prussian blue, hazy cerulean |
| Warm darks (#1A1917) | deep umber, warm charcoal, near-black earth |
| Creams (#F7F7F5) | warm ivory, unbleached linen, paper white |

Name the brand accent as the *light source or focal accent* ("golden-hour amber light") and the neutrals as the *ground* — that's how a palette becomes a painting instead of a swatch list.
